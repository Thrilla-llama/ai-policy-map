Marietta City Schools

The school board voted on AI and screen rules. Screens stay closed unless there is a clear reason. AI is not allowed in grades K–5. In middle and high school, assignments use red / yellow / green labels, and MagicSchool is used with limits. Leaders talked with families and teachers first.

The gap

  • The board voted on screens and AI. Screens stay closed unless there is a clear purpose.
  • Generative AI is banned in grades K–5. Middle and high school assignments use red / yellow / green labels.
  • MagicSchool is the district-supported tool. ChatGPT is not on the approved list for schoolwork.

Classroom AI rules

Published traffic-light (mapped). Mapped from Marietta’s published traffic-light framing (board vision on screens/AI; MagicSchool). Not an official district handout.

Approved for schoolwork: MagicSchool AI. Not approved unless a teacher lists an exception: ChatGPT and other open consumer chatbots.

ColorMeaningStudents mayStudents must
Green — AI OKAI is allowed as a helperUse MagicSchool (or another tool the teacher names) to brainstorm, outline, practice, or get feedbackSay what they used and how; final thinking in their own words
Yellow — AI limitedAI only for named stepsUse AI only for the steps the teacher listsFollow those limits; no AI on the graded part unless listed
Red — no AIThis work is unaidedNot use generative AI for any part of the assignmentAsk if unsure; AI here counts as academic dishonesty

If no color is marked, treat the assignment as Yellow until the teacher says otherwise. Screens closed by default — AI colors do not override screen rules. K–5: treat student generative AI as Red.

Open one-page classroom rules

What helps / What hurts

What helps

  • The board posted a public vote on AI and screen use — families can point to it.
  • Secondary classes can label homework red, yellow, or green for AI use, so expectations are clearer.
  • Teachers have MagicSchool as a known AI tool in staff and secondary settings.
  • Teachers: The board vote plus traffic-light labels give a shared signal; MagicSchool is available, though training is only as-needed.

What hurts

  • AI is banned in K–5, and we found no public plan to teach younger students AI skills.
  • Parent AI updates so far are listening sessions, not a finished family guide.
  • The district has not published results on how these rules are working.
  • Students: The K–5 ban and screens-closed default put safety first over early AI literacy; secondary students get clearer labels when traffic-light rules are used.

These points come from public district sources we checked. Empty fields elsewhere mean we don’t know yet — we do not invent missing facts.
