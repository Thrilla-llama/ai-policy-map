# District AI policy glossary (deep)

For each code: **What it means**, **How it’s usually communicated**, **What ends up happening** (students / teachers / parents).

---

## `ai_policy_status`

### `none`
- **What it means:** No public AI policy, guidance, principles, or handbook language found.
- **How communicated:** Silence — or only informal verbal guidance from a school.
- **What ends up happening:** Teachers invent different rules; students get conflicting answers; parents can’t cite an official stance. Pressure point: ask board/superintendent for written guidance.

### `unknown`
- **What it means:** We haven’t verified yet (or sources were inaccessible).
- **How communicated:** N/A in product until verified.
- **What ends up happening:** Treat like `none` for practical purposes until filled.

### `principles_only`
- **What it means:** The district published **values / principles** (e.g. “use AI responsibly,” “protect privacy,” “academic integrity”) **without** concrete classroom rules, allowed tools, or assignment procedures. “Values only” = aspiration, not an operating manual.
- **How communicated:** Often a short web page, slide deck, board presentation, or “AI principles” PDF — not a numbered policy with enforcement.
- **What ends up happening:**
  - **Students:** Hear “be careful with AI” but not whether ChatGPT on homework is allowed.
  - **Teachers:** Must invent their own rules; inconsistency across classrooms is the norm.
  - **Parents:** Can quote principles in advocacy, but can’t point to a binding rule yet. Next ask: turn principles into handbook language + assignment framework.

### `resolution`
- **What it means:** Board voted a formal **resolution** stating a stance (support, caution, pilot, etc.) without necessarily adopting a full policy.
- **How communicated:** Board agenda / minutes / resolution PDF.
- **What ends up happening:** Political signal exists; day-to-day practice may still look like `teacher_discretion` until procedures follow. Parents can cite the vote date and text.

### `guidance`
- **What it means:** Written guidance for educators/students (how-to, expectations) that is **not** (or not yet) adopted as board policy.
- **How communicated:** Staff portal PDFs, instructional technology pages, student guidance docs, sometimes linked from curriculum sites.
- **What ends up happening:** Many teachers treat it as the real rulebook; students follow if teachers share it; enforceability varies. Stronger than principles; weaker than board policy for accountability.

### `drafting_guidance`
- **What it means:** Guidance is being written; not final.
- **How communicated:** Drafts, committee notes, “coming soon,” internal share-outs.
- **What ends up happening:** Interim chaos — some schools use drafts, others wait. Ask what’s in force **this semester**.

### `drafting_IFBG`
- **What it means:** A formal AI / acceptable-use style board policy (often coded IFBG or similar) is in the pipeline.
- **How communicated:** Board policy drafts, first/second reads, Simbli/BoardDocs packets.
- **What ends up happening:** Watch board calendars; once adopted it becomes `board_policy`. Until then, interim handbook/guidance may still apply.

### `board_policy`
- **What it means:** Adopted board policy governing AI (or AI-relevant tech use).
- **How communicated:** Official policy manual (BoardDocs/Simbli/district policy site).
- **What ends up happening:** Clearest compliance baseline. Students/teachers can be held to it; parents have a durable document to cite.

### `procedure_handbook`
- **What it means:** Rules live in **student/staff handbook** and/or administrative procedures (academic honesty, device use, AI misuse).
- **How communicated:** Annual handbook PDF, Code of Conduct, procedure manuals — often signed by families.
- **What ends up happening:** This is where discipline and grade consequences usually live. Even without a fancy “AI policy,” handbook language can ban unauthorized AI or deepfakes. Parents: read the honesty / tech sections.

## `assignment_framework`

### `teacher_discretion`
- **What it means:** No district-wide assignment rule; each teacher decides.
- **How communicated:** Syllabus, LMS assignment notes, verbal instructions — inconsistent.
- **What ends up happening:** Student can be fine in period 1 and in trouble in period 2 for the same ChatGPT use. Teachers own clarity; parents should ask for written assignment expectations.

### `traffic_light`
- **What it means:** District (or school) uses levels — e.g. red = no AI, yellow = limited/with disclosure, green = AI expected/encouraged.
- **How communicated:** Rubrics, posters, assignment tags, PD materials.
- **What ends up happening:** Students need the light for **this** task; teachers should label every major assignment. Parents can ask “what color is this homework?”

### `citation_required`
- **What it means:** AI assistance allowed if the student discloses/cites it.
- **How communicated:** Honor code addenda, assignment templates, syllabus integrity statements.
- **What ends up happening:** Undisclosed AI = academic honesty issue. Teachers grade process + product; students must show their work/disclosure.

### `unknown`
- **What it means:** No clear published framework.
- **How communicated:** Silence.
- **What ends up happening:** Same practical risk as `teacher_discretion` without even teacher norms — ask before using AI.

## `elementary_ai` / `secondary_ai` (classroom use bands)

### `banned`
- **Means:** AI tools not allowed at this level.
- **Communicated:** Handbook, board policy, grade-band rules.
- **Happens:** Students shouldn’t use; teachers prohibit; enforcement via honesty/tech policies.

### `restricted`
- **Means:** Only narrow, approved cases (e.g. IEP tools, teacher-led demos).
- **Communicated:** Guidance lists / approved-tools lists.
- **Happens:** Default is no; exceptions must be explicit.

### `teacher_discretion`
- **Means:** Teacher decides for that grade band.
- **Communicated:** Classroom rules.
- **Happens:** High variance by teacher.

### `permitted_with_citation`
- **Means:** Allowed with disclosure.
- **Communicated:** Assignment framework + syllabus.
- **Happens:** Disclose or face honesty consequences.

### `unknown`
- **Means:** Not published.
- **Happens:** Ask school leadership.

## `teacher_pd`

| Code | Means | Communicated | What happens |
|------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| `none` | No AI PD found | — | Teachers self-teach; inconsistent practice |
| `unknown` | Not verified | — | — |

| `cohort` | Structured group/cohort training | Program pages | Shared practice emerging |

| Code | Means | Communicated | What happens |

| `unknown` | Not verified | — | — |

## `safety_ai_status` (AI harm / deepfake / impersonation)

Separate from `ai_policy_status` (assignment clarity). Answers: does the LEA ban **weaponized AI**?

### `none`
No public rule on deepfakes, AI impersonation, or AI-facilitated harassment.

### `procedure_handbook`
Code of Conduct / handbook bans unauthorized AI deepfakes, false images, or similar.

### `guidance`
Educator/student guidance or position statement includes a safety section (Accuracy/Integrity/Safety, deepfake warnings, etc.).

### `board_policy`
Adopted board policy (often IFBG/IFBI or AUP) addresses AI safety harms and/or approved-tools-only with human oversight.

### Flags
See `risk_impact_schema.md` — `deepfake_ban`, `impersonation_ban`, `links_to_hib_titleix`, `approved_tools_only` = `yes` | `no` | `unknown`.

## `graduation_evidence` / `attendance_evidence` (S/G/A)

Pete rank order: **Safety > Graduation > Attendance**.

### Enums
- `none` — no public AI-linked evidence
- `narrative` — claim only, no metrics
- `metrics` — published numbers tying AI work to recovery/completion (G) or attendance/engagement (A)
- `unknown` — not verified

Grad-rate theater without an AI link does **not** count. Never invent.
